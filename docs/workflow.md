# Workflow

## Overview

The Nyxthorh Lab is developed using an iterative engineering workflow focused on planning, implementation, validation, and continuous improvement.

Rather than aiming for a perfect solution from the beginning, the environment evolves through incremental changes supported by documentation and regular review.

The workflow is intended to encourage learning while maintaining a stable and manageable infrastructure.

---

## Objectives

The primary objectives of the workflow are to:

- Establish a structured approach to infrastructure development.
- Reduce unnecessary operational risk.
- Document architectural decisions as they are made.
- Validate changes before they become permanent.
- Encourage continuous learning through practical experience.
- Maintain a consistent engineering process across all projects.

The workflow prioritises understanding and maintainability over implementation speed.

---

## Design

Development follows a repeatable process where each change begins with a clearly defined objective.

Solutions are planned before implementation, evaluated after deployment, and documented throughout the process. This approach makes it easier to identify successful design decisions while capturing lessons learned from unexpected outcomes.

Maintaining a consistent workflow also simplifies future improvements by ensuring that projects evolve in a predictable manner.

---

## Operational Philosophy

Infrastructure development is viewed as an iterative process rather than a sequence of isolated tasks.

Every implementation provides an opportunity to improve technical understanding, refine existing designs, and strengthen documentation.

The workflow encourages deliberate decision-making, continuous evaluation, and incremental improvements instead of large, disruptive changes.

Consistency is considered more valuable than rapid implementation.

---

## Lessons Learned

Following a structured workflow has consistently reduced unnecessary complexity and improved the overall quality of the environment.

Planning before implementation, documenting decisions, and reviewing completed work has made future maintenance significantly easier.

One of the most valuable lessons has been that a repeatable engineering process often contributes more to long-term success than any individual technology used within the infrastructure.
